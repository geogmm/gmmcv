About me.

Understanding how cities
and regions evolve.

I am a PhD candidate at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. My research interests lie at the intersection of economic geography, innovation studies, and urban networks. I combine large-scale data analysis with econometric modelling and causal inference to investigate how cities and regions evolve.

Before beginning my doctoral studies, I spent five years as a senior urban planning engineer in China’s public sector, focusing on high-tech industry and land-use planning. This experience gave me firsthand insight into how cities and regions pursue industrial upgrading and sustain economic growth. It also motivated me to pursue academic research that connects rigorous empirical analysis with practical insights for regional policymaking.

I am actively exploring how AI can reshape the way I conduct research, from formulating questions to analysing complex spatial and economic processes. In my spare time, I am developing a multi-agent AI system to explore urban evolution through spatial, temporal, and relational data (UrbanAgent ↗). Through this project, I aim to create an open, reproducible research platform that integrates open data, computational modelling, and AI.

Ideas in conversation

Talks.

December 01, 2019, Foshan, Guangdong Province, China

Industrial Innovation Planning in the Pearl River Delta

This talk provides an overview of the historical development, emerging opportunities, key challenges, and major planning insights for the high-tech industry in the Pearl River Delta. Drawing on years of practical experience in regional planning, the presentation examines how the PRD has evolved into a global manufacturing and innovation hub, the policy and institutional factors that have shaped its industrial trajectory, and the spatial and economic disparities that pose challenges to its future development. Key planning insights emphasize the importance of fostering innovation ecosystems, promoting industrial upgrading, and addressing regional inequalities to sustain long-term growth.

Data & research infrastructure

Datasets.

Multi-agent spatial research

UrbanAgent ↗

UrbanAgent is a multi-agent system I am developing to bridge AI and urban research. It ingests user-uploaded spatial data and autonomously uncovers spatial relationships, interprets spatial evolution, and generates forecasts. The system comprises multiple specialised agents: a data-reading agent, a spatial-relationship analysis agent, a spatial modelling agent, and a spatial prediction agent. By orchestrating these agents, UrbanAgent aims to offer a scalable, interpretable tool for researchers and policymakers to understand urban dynamics and anticipate future spatial patterns.

01. Global Patent Database

Coverage
Global patent records, with more than 120 million observations collected from Google Patents.

Patent identifiers; dates; applicants and inventors; technology classifications; titles and abstracts; priority information; citation information; and other bibliographic metadata.

02. China Patent Transfer Database

Coverage
Comprehensive patent transfer records in China from 1986 to 2025.
Key variables
Patent information; transfer date; transferor; transferee; transferor address; transferee address; geographic information; and associated patent characteristics.

03. China Trademark Database

Coverage
Comprehensive trademark registration records in China from 2000 to 2025.
Key variables
Trademark identifiers; applicants / registrants; applicant addresses; trademark status; goods and services categories; and related registration information.

04. Hong Kong Trademark Database

Coverage
Hong Kong trademark registrations from 2010 to 2025, based on records from the Hong Kong Intellectual Property Department.
Key variables
Trademark identifiers; application and registration dates; registrants; registrant addresses; trademark classifications; goods and services categories; and geographic information.

Data Access and Research Collaboration

These databases were independently collected, processed, standardised, and structured for academic research. They can be used individually or combined with other spatial, economic, demographic, patent, firm, and urban datasets to investigate a wide range of questions related to innovation, technology transfer, knowledge flows, entrepreneurship, and regional development.

If you are interested in using any of these datasets, please feel free to contact me to discuss data access and potential research collaborations. I am also happy to discuss potential research questions and empirical projects that can be developed using these data.
